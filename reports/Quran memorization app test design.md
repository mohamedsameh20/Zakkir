# Build Hifz Self-Testing Around Manual Reveal

Every serious hifz app treats the self-test as a loop: hide the text in place on a fixed mushaf page, let the reciter uncover it a word or an ayah at a time, then record which words or ayat failed. Tarteel drives that loop with server-side speech recognition and treats manual reveal as a penalized fallback ("each reveal counts as a mistake"). The best non-AI designs, though, show that the loop works without a microphone. Those designs are Quran.com's own unmerged Hifz-mode PR for Quran for Android and the open-source Quran Revision Companion. They use the same controls: reveal-next-word and reveal-next-ayah buttons side by side, an undo, tap-a-word to flag a mistake, and a binary Yes/No verdict. For Zakkir, this means the owner's product decisions are well supported by precedent: binary grading, a session score, per-ayah and per-word history, the exact 604-page Madani layout, and local-only data. The hard parts are not the UI. They are three things. First, licensing: free per-ayah audio CDNs publish no license. The Quran Foundation API forbids bundling and needs a backend Zakkir does not want. QUL's layout data has no stated data license. Second, rendering 604 per-page QCF fonts in the WebView Zakkir already uses. Third, a quiet storage trap: Android Auto Backup stops backing up an app whose data passes 25 MB, so downloaded audio or fonts could silently disable backup of the mistake history. This report compares how apps test, grade, display mistakes, play audio, render the mushaf and schedule revision. It then proposes a concrete control set, data sources and a mistake schema for Zakkir.

## Word and ayah reveal sit side by side, not behind a mode switch

The most useful finding for Zakkir's "flexible granularity" requirement is that both reference designs expose word-level and ayah-level reveal **at the same time, as adjacent buttons**, rather than asking the user to pick a mode first. Tarteel's Testing Mode was added in August 2026. It presents a "question card" at a random point in the user's memorized range, with a few hint words as a starting prompt. When the reciter is stuck, a **single arrow reveals one word, a double arrow reveals "the rest of the ayah"**, and a double-tap on the card reveals too. An eye icon toggles visibility and "Next" skips ([Tarteel Help: Getting started with Testing](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/16557456-getting-started-with-testing)). Quran.com's Quran for Android has an open issue (July 2026) and an **open, unmerged PR #3824 (September 2026)** for a Hifz mode. They specify five 48dp buttons in a floating bar: `<<` re-hide verse, `<` re-hide word, eye toggle, `>` reveal word, `>>` reveal verse. Tapping a hidden verse reveals the whole verse. Verse-number markers stay visible, and **audio is disabled "to prevent the user from hearing the answer"** ([Issue #3749](https://github.com/quran/quran_android/issues/3749); [PR #3824](https://github.com/quran/quran_android/pull/3824/files)). Under the hood, PR #3824 is a pure state machine (`revealNextWord`, `revealNextVerse`, `rehideLastWord`, `rehideLastVerse`) over a reveal stack that gives undo for free. It is the only design anywhere in this research with an undo control, and it ports almost line for line to TypeScript.

Tarteel's older hidden mode is AI-first. In Recite mode an eye-slash icon "blanks out ALL the verses on the page and leaves the Ayah markers alone". Words reappear as the AI hears them, and an option hides the markers too ([Tarteel Help: Hide ayahs](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/12414416-hide-ayahs)). The only manual aid is a "peek" at the next word or the next whole ayah, colored brown and logged as a mistake ([Tarteel Help: text colours](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/12414414-what-do-the-different-text-colours-mean)). **Testing "needs an internet connection, because your recitation is recognized live on our servers"** ([Testing FAQs](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/16557351-testing-faqs)), and I found no documented mic-off or self-graded mode. Quran.com web has no hide-words feature at all as of October 2026. Its memorization support is audio repeat, notes and reading goals ([quran.com-frontend-next](https://github.com/quran/quran.com-frontend-next)).

Outside those two, genuinely self-graded tests are rare. The open-source **Quran Revision Companion (Waqar144)** is the cleanest non-AI precedent. It has two quiz prompts, "Recite the next ayah" and "Finish the ayah" (which hides the last `min(ceil(words/2), 6)` words), and a "mix" of both. One "Show Answer" button reveals the target. A binary **"Were you right?" No/Yes** feeds "Your score is X/Y", and wrong ayat can be pushed to a revision list. On the mushaf, **tapping a word toggles it as a mistake** ([quran_memorization_helper](https://github.com/Waqar144/quran_memorization_helper)). Other apps contribute single ideas worth borrowing. Quran Companion reveals in proportion to a finger swipe, and swiping back re-hides ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quran-companion-memorize-quran/id1111843462)). CandanUmut's open-source *hifz* fades ink through first letters and word-shaped "ghosts" to blank space, and **every 1.8-second peek caps the item's grade** ([CandanUmut/hifz](https://github.com/CandanUmut/hifz)). Tasmiq uses a three-step hint ladder of juz, then surah name, then a growing fragment of the answer, and counts mistakes and prompts separately ([Tasmiq](https://github.com/xebec51/quran-memorization-app)). The Quranki Anki deck **splits ayat at waqf marks into 10,521 cards averaging 7.3 words instead of 6,236 averaging 12.5**, a unit between word and ayah ([Quranki](https://github.com/Nasr-905/Quranki)).

## Mistakes live on the page as colour, and fade with clean recitations

Apps record mistakes at word level but display them in two time frames. **Live**, Tarteel colours words red (wrong, missing or extra), yellow (tashkeel), brown (peeked) and green (correct), and adds a dotted underline that disappears once corrected ([How to activate Mistake Detection](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/12414419-how-to-activate-mistake-detection)). **Historically**, a separate heatmap paints each ayah red, orange, yellow or green by mistake frequency. The colour **steps back down as the user recites the ayah correctly again**, "from red down to green, and eventually to no highlight". All mistake types, including peeks and diacritic errors, count toward it ([Understanding mistake highlight colors](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/12414495-understanding-mistake-highlight-colors)). The exact decay rate is undocumented. Tarteel adds two details that matter even more in a self-graded app, where the user will mis-tap. Any flagged mistake can be **removed and later restored**, and removed mistakes stop counting toward history, highlights and scores ([A mistake was flagged that I didn't make](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/16559266-a-mistake-was-flagged-that-i-didn-t-make)). A **"Temporary Session"** toggle also runs a test that saves nothing ([Testing FAQs](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/16557351-testing-faqs)). The non-AI equivalent is simpler. Quran Revision Companion draws marked words in red on a light-red background and lists "marked ayahs" per juz ([repo source](https://github.com/Waqar144/quran_memorization_helper)). A 2016 request on Quran for Android asked for exactly Tarteel's later model: per-ayah markers that darken with each mistake and lighten when recited correctly ([Issue #702](https://github.com/quran/quran_android/issues/702)).

Teachers classify mistakes differently from AI. They grade **who intervened**, not just what was wrong. The Ahl al-Quran exam platform deducts in three categories: تلقيني (the committee supplied the text), تنبيهي (alerted, then self-corrected) and تجويدي (tajweed). Its bands are retake below 70, then 70–80, 80–90 and 90–100 ([Ahl al-Quran help](https://help.ehlquran.com/ar/articles/8570962-%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%AA%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A2%D9%86)). Al-Azhar's international competition **alerts the student twice before supplying the text (فتح) at a one-mark cost**, and scores hesitation separately ([Al-Azhar](https://azhar.eg/details/ArtMID/821/ArticleID/41603/%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%8A%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%82%D9%8A%D9%8A%D9%85-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%82%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B2%D9%87%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A2%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%85)). Severity splits into لحن جلي, which changes the word or its meaning, and لحن خفي, a tajweed fault ([Nabulsi](https://nabulsi.com/web/article/11280)). Mutashabihat confusion is a category of its own. Open data maps confusable ayah pairs by absolute ayah number ([Waqar144/Quran_Mutashabihat_Data](https://github.com/Waqar144/Quran_Mutashabihat_Data)), and the iOS tracker Rasekh lets users tag words with tajweed issues, hesitation or forgotten verses ([Rasekh](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rasekh-quran-hifz-tracker/id6766639262)).

For Zakkir the lesson is that the teacher's distinction between a **hint** and a **failure** carries more information than any AI category. A binary right/wrong verdict loses nothing important as long as the app separately and automatically records whether hints or peeks were used. Typed categories can be added later without changing the binary verdict. No standardized cross-organization taxonomy exists, and I found no evidence on how reliable self-grading is compared with teacher grading.

## Audio licensing, not playback code, is the hard part

Hifz audio features have converged on a common baseline: a reciter picker, verse repeat count including infinite, range repeat count, a delay between repeats, speed, offline download, and follow-along highlighting. Quran.com's player groups speed, reciter, download and repeat settings ([Quran.com product update](https://quran.com/en/product-updates/simplifying-word-by-word-and-audio-settings)). Quran for iOS made repeat counts persist between sessions, storing endless as 0 ([quran-ios PR #1036](https://github.com/quran/quran-ios/pull/1036)). Users on Quran.com's own app ask to "repeat & loop" ayat and complain that "audio cut between each ayht" ([justuseapp Quran.com reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1118663303/quran-by-quran-com-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A2%D9%86/reviews)). The repeat-with-pause pattern is an old Quran.com issue, which also notes that a partial-ayah repeat was once removed ([Issue #1022](https://github.com/quran/quran.com-frontend-next/issues/1022)).

**Per-ayah files make repeat logic trivial.** EveryAyah serves `everyayah.com/data/{Reciter_Bitrate}/SSSAAA.mp3` with a full-set zip and an MD5 checksum file per reciter, at 32–192 kbps depending on reciter ([EveryAyah](https://everyayah.com/recitations_ayat.html)). The islamic.network CDN serves `cdn.islamic.network/quran/audio/{bitrate}/{edition}/{1..6236}.mp3` at 32–192 kbps ([alquran.cloud CDN](https://alquran.cloud/cdn)). mp3quran.net offers only per-surah files, but adds per-ayah millisecond timings through `ayat_timing` ([mp3quran API](https://www.mp3quran.net/eng/api)). **None of these three publishes a license or terms of use.** The Quran Foundation API is the only source with explicit terms. Those terms allow free, paid and ad-supported apps with the attribution "Quran data provided by Quran Foundation" ([QF FAQ](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/docs/tutorials/faq/)). But they cap caching at one week, outside a Content Sync exception that returns audio URLs rather than files. They also state that **"a browser or mobile app must not embed a Content API `client_secret`… call your own backend"** ([QF Quickstart](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/docs/quickstart/)). That conflicts head-on with Zakkir's no-server, offline-download decisions. QUL (Tarteel's Quranic Universal Library) offers 133 recitations, 59 of them with word-level segments, as JSON or SQLite ([QUL recitations](https://qul.tarteel.ai/resources/recitation)). But it states no per-resource license, and a GitHub issue asking exactly whether a free offline app may bundle the data is unanswered ([QUL issue #772](https://github.com/TarteelAI/quranic-universal-library/issues/772)). Word timings are tied to one specific recording, so segment data cannot be mixed with audio from another source unless the files are identical.

On the client side, Zakkir has a version problem. Current expo-audio supports background playback through `enableBackgroundPlayback`, gapless `useAudioPlaylist` with loop modes, and a 0.1–2.0 playback rate. But **Android playback "will stop after approximately 3 minutes" unless `setActiveForLockScreen` is enabled** ([Expo audio docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/audio/)). The changelog suggests lock-screen controls in the SDK 53 era were iOS-only ([expo-audio CHANGELOG](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-54/packages/expo-audio/CHANGELOG.md)), and that is unverified for Android. A recent PR changes the background default to false ([expo PR #49880](https://github.com/expo/expo/pull/49880)). react-native-track-player v5 has a Media3 queue, caching and a native sleep timer, but **v5 is commercially licensed**, and v4 remains Apache-2.0 but is legacy ([RNTP repo](https://github.com/doublesymmetry/react-native-track-player)). Background playback on Android 14+ also needs the `mediaPlayback` foreground-service declaration in Play Console, including a demo video ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821?hl=en)). A rough, unmeasured estimate puts one full reciter at 0.3–1.7 GB depending on bitrate. That figure matters below for backups.

## Page-exact rendering needs 604 fonts and line data that the QF API cannot ship offline

Huffaz recall by position, so fidelity to the printed page is not cosmetic. A hifz app spec states it directly: "every Line to start and end on the same word as my printed… Mushaf", with the whole page visible without scrolling ([hafiz-quran issue #10](https://github.com/AxeemHaider/hafiz-quran/issues/10)). The standard way to get this is the **King Fahd Complex QCF fonts: one font file per page, 604 per version, where each glyph is a whole pre-shaped, kashida-stretched word** exactly as printed on that page ([QF Font Rendering guide](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/docs/tutorials/fonts/font-rendering/); [QUL glyph docs](https://qul.tarteel.ai/docs/glyph-based)). V2 corresponds to the current 1421H print, and V1 to the 1405H print. They paginate differently, so V2 is the right default unless users memorized from the older print ([QUL Mushaf Layouts](https://qul.tarteel.ai/resources/mushaf-layout)). Measured V2 woff2 pages run from **41 KB to 190 KB**, and one project puts the full V2 woff2 set at **about 45 MB** ([furqan-app #601](https://github.com/furqan-app/web/issues/601)). Line placement comes from QUL's layout database. Its `pages` table has one row per line, with `line_type` (ayah, surah_name, basmallah), `is_centered` and an inclusive `first_word_id..last_word_id` range, joined to a glyph-code script keyed by word ([QUL Mushaf layout docs](https://qul.tarteel.ai/docs/mushaf-layout)). The single Unicode KFGQPC HAFS font is far smaller. But even with forced line breaks it is "line-exact", not "glyph-exact", and practitioners argue such pages never match the print ([nedaa.dev](https://nedaa.dev/docs/building-a-quran-app/)).

The WebView Zakkir already uses is the better host for this. In HTML each word is a span, so **hiding a word with `color: transparent` or `visibility: hidden` keeps its advance width and the justified line does not move**. Replacing it with a placeholder would re-justify the line and break fidelity. Full-line justification uses `text-align: justify` plus `text-align-last: justify`, with `font-size: 0` on the line container to kill inter-span whitespace ([quranportal.io](https://quranportal.io/blog/rendering-the-quran-mushaf-digitally)). SakinaDevGroup's `al_quran_madani` is a dependency-free `index.html` built to run in a WebView, with each word a `<span>` carrying `data-surah`, `data-ayah` and `data-page`. It is a near-ready reference at 46 MB including V1 fonts ([SakinaDevGroup/al_quran_madani](https://github.com/SakinaDevGroup/al_quran_madani)). The native alternative has a documented failure mode. An Expo Quran app that registers one `expo-font` family per page found that **expo-font cannot unload fonts**, which accumulates toward a ~120 MB ceiling across the book ([J3ff4/quran-corpus #106](https://github.com/J3ff4/quran-corpus/issues/106)). In a WebView, `FontFace` objects for the current page plus neighbours can be added and dropped freely.

Licensing determines which files can actually ship. The King Fahd Complex grants free use of its mushaf fonts "in websites, software, and other similar intermediates" ([KFGQPC copyright page, archived 2019](http://web.archive.org/web/20190819034348/https://dm.qurancomplex.gov.sa/copyright-2/)). Its font EULA permits use, copying and distribution but forbids selling, modifying or reverse-engineering ([Open Hub KFGQPC](https://openhub.net/licenses/KFGQPC)). A different notice on the HAFS Unicode font says it "may not be reproduced, modified" without written approval ([ScanCode LicenseDB](https://scancode-licensedb.aboutcode.org/kfgqpc-uthmanic-script-hafs.html)). That conflict is unresolved. The live KFGQPC site refused connections during research, and whether converting TTF to woff2 counts as forbidden "modification" is a grey area. Tanzil text is CC BY 3.0 but adds "verbatim only, CHANGING IT IS NOT ALLOWED" plus attribution with a link to tanzil.net ([Tanzil Text License](https://tanzil.net/docs/Text_License)). The Quran Foundation **explicitly forbids distributing "a prepackaged database or build-time bundle of QF Content"**. It allows bundled fonts only with an active Developer Console account and credit ([QF Mushaf Fonts and Images terms](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/legal/mushaf-fonts-and-images/)). QUL's code is MIT, but its data license is unstated.

## Revision runs on three tiers that predate every app

Apps borrowed the structure of revision from tradition. South Asian sabaq / sabqi / manzil and Arab جديد / مراجعة قريبة / مراجعة بعيدة both run three tiers every day. The tiers are new material, a sliding window of recent material, and a rotating long-term cycle, often **one juz a day, so a full khatma roughly monthly** ([The Hifz Project](https://thehifzproject.com/articles/sabaq-sabqi-manzil)). Sources disagree on the recent window: one cites the last 7–15 days ([Islamic Tuition](https://islamictuition.us/blog/sabaq-sabqi-manzil-hifz-revision-system/)), and another 20–30 days ([The Hifz Project](https://thehifzproject.com/articles/sabaq-sabqi-manzil)). The Five Fortresses method uses the 20 pages adjacent to the new page ([Islamweb fatwa 157229](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/157229/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%B5%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A9)). So the window has to be configurable. The pedagogical rule is strict: new material **"must be error-free before it counts"**, because "a lesson accepted with mistakes becomes a mistake memorized" ([The Hifz Project](https://thehifzproject.com/articles/sabaq-sabqi-manzil)).

Apps layer measurement and scheduling on top of this. Tarteel's goals have Memorize, Review and Recite types over a range, a portion and a schedule. **Only sessions with hidden ayat count toward Memorize and Review** ([Tarteel Goals](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/12782033-how-do-i-use-the-goals-feature)). That is a rule worth copying, because it keeps reading from passing as testing. Its "Suggested for you" cards include a fixed **Warm Up** (most confident passages) and **Weakest Link** (most mistakes), both built from review history ([Suggested tests](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/16558156-what-are-the-suggested-for-you-tests)). Quran.com's streak needs only "at least a verse daily" ([Quran.com product update](https://quran.com/product-updates/quran-reading-streaks)). The open-source AHD planner instead requires all three tiers green. It keeps a 0–100 per-page strength that rises on clean recitations, falls on mistakes and **decays when a page goes about 40 days untouched**, scheduled by an SM-2 variant ([AHD](https://github.com/Bahtiyorjon05/Quran-learning-plan-)). Rasekh pulls shaky surahs forward within days while leaving the rotation intact ([Rasekh](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rasekh-quran-hifz-tracker/id6766639262)). Practitioners say flashcard SRS works for Quran only when the unit is a page or passage prompted by the preceding text, and that it "requires a number of tweaks" ([HowToMemoriseTheQuran](https://howtomemorisethequran.com/using-flashcards-for-hifz-quran-memorisation/)). FSRS, native in Anki since 23.10, needs a review log of graded attempts ([fsrs4anki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki)). That is the data Zakkir would have to keep from day one to add it later.

Hybrid scheduling is the defensible synthesis. A classical manzil rotation is the floor, and weakness scores pull weak pages forward. Per-ayah and per-word detail is aggregated to pages for scheduling but kept for diagnosis. The progress views apps converge on are a 30-juz grid drilling down to a 604-page mosaic (AHD's "Mushaf Mosaic"), a calendar heatmap of review days, a weakest-ayat list linking straight to a targeted test, and the mushaf page tinted by mistake count ([AHD](https://github.com/Bahtiyorjon05/Quran-learning-plan-); [Hifz Hero](https://github.com/HibaChamkhi/hifdh_hero)). No published study compares FSRS or SM-2 with traditional rotation for Quran retention. The evidence is anecdotal.

## Users punish paywalls, false flags and reflowed pages

The sentiment evidence (App Store reviews, aggregators and comparison blogs; Reddit and Play review text were unreachable) points to one opening for Zakkir. Tarteel is the most loved and most criticized app, and the criticism clusters on things a manual app avoids by construction. Users object that core memorization tools are premium-only ("what use is a Quran app...without ability to correct mistakes"). They find it "so frustrating to dismiss premium popup every time" ([Tarteel App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tarteel-ai-quran-memorization/id1391009396?see-all=reviews)), at a listed $9.99/month or $250 lifetime ([howtomemorisethequran.com](https://howtomemorisethequran.com/top-quran-memorization-apps/)). A premium reviewer reports the AI "revisits words it previously marked correct", and others say "it stops listening to me as I am reading it for Hifz" ([justuseapp Tarteel](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1391009396/tarteel-recite-al-quran/reviews)). A competitor's analysis of 500 recent US reviews found reports of false "you made a mistake" interruptions. The same developer's own controlled test found no false flags, and both sources are interested parties ([Mizan PR #285](https://github.com/ProfAlfailakawi/Mizan/pull/285)).

The steelman against manual apps is real. Reviewers list "Cannot verify you actually said the verse correctly" as Quran Companion's main con ([quranicma.com](https://quranicma.com/best-quran-memorization-apps-2026/)). A two-year daily Tarteel user warns that users become dependent on the screen ([Tarteel App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tarteel-ai-quran-memorization/id1391009396?see-all=reviews)). Neither is framed as a dealbreaker, and the manual reveal itself is praised: "the swipe option helps you check if you have correctly memorized it" ([Quran Companion](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quran-companion-memorize-quran/id1111843462)). The design response is honesty tooling: count hints, show them in the score, and offer a strict mode. I found no direct user quotes demanding a no-mic app for privacy or quiet places, so that motivation is plausible but unconfirmed.

The remaining pain points are mechanical, and each maps to a Zakkir requirement. Audio "doesn't go along with the screen cursor" ([justuseapp Quran.com](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1118663303/quran-by-quran-com-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A2%D9%86/reviews)). Reflowing apps "become useless for revising from memory" ([dralirajabi.com](https://dralirajabi.com/checklist-for-choosing-quran-for-memorization/)). Ayat "can't read offline (Android)", and ad-heavy apps are "plastered with ads" ([howtomemorisethequran.com](https://howtomemorisethequran.com/top-quran-memorization-apps/)). There are also unverified Play snippets about lost settings and resume-to-page-one after reinstall ([Google Play snippet](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.greentech.quran&hl=en_US)). Mutashabihat are called "the biggest stumbling blocks when revising" ([quransheikh.com](https://www.quransheikh.com/how-to-memorize-quran-without-forgetting/)).

## Design recommendations for Zakkir

### A control bar with two reveal buttons, a hint ladder and an undo

The recommended control set combines Quran for Android's button bar, Quran Revision Companion's tap-to-flag and binary verdict, and Tarteel's session wrapper, with hints separated from reveals. The core idea is that **revealing is neutral, because revealing is how you check**, while hints and peeks are recorded as evidence and the verdict is the user's. That fixes the flaw in Tarteel's "each reveal = a mistake" rule, which cannot tell "I knew it and checked" from "I forgot". Granularity is switched by which button you press, at any moment, including mid-ayah. A word-by-word start can be finished with "rest of ayah", exactly as Tarteel's double arrow does.

| Control | Behaviour | What it records | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Veil (eye)** | Hides all text on the page in place (transparent glyphs, faint word "pills"), markers stay; optional "hide ayah markers" | Press-and-hold to view the page = `peek` | Tarteel Hide Ayahs, Quran for Android |
| **Reveal word `>`** | Reveals the next hidden word in reading order | Neutral; counts words revealed singly | Tarteel single arrow, PR #3824 |
| **Reveal ayah `>>`** | Reveals the rest of the current ayah | Neutral | Tarteel double arrow, PR #3824 |
| **Tap page** | Repeats the last-used reveal unit (big target for one-handed use) | Same as the button it repeats | Tarteel double-tap card, PR #3824 tap-verse |
| **Hint** | Ladder on the next hidden word: first letter / word shape → meaning (word-by-word gloss) → transliteration → (later) word audio. A separate rung shows the first word of the next ayah | `hint` with highest rung reached | CandanUmut fade, Tasmiq ladder, Tarteel peek |
| **Undo `<`** (long-press = `<<`) | Re-hides the last revealed unit, or the whole last-touched ayah; also clears that unit's grade | Removes the event | PR #3824 reveal stack |
| **✓ / ✗** | Appear when an ayah is fully revealed; binary verdict for that ayah | `result` per ayah | Quran Revision Companion Yes/No |
| **Tap revealed word** | Toggles that word as wrong; any flagged word pre-selects ✗ for its ayah | Per-word `wrong` mark | Quran Revision Companion, Rasekh |
| **Long-press word** | Sheet with gloss and transliteration; tag "confused with ayah…" | Optional `confused_with` | Waqar144 mutashabihat, Mualim Binder |
| **Word-drill setting** | Replaces Reveal with "✓ next" / "✗ next": one tap grades the shown word and reveals the next | Per-word right/wrong | Synthesis for short surahs and learners |
| **Session wrapper** | Scope (surah / juz / hizb / page / range / "my memorization"), kind (page veil or random-start prompt with N hint words and L lines extended to ayah end), length, temporary-session toggle; summary with score, flagged words, hints, wrong ayat → "add to revision", Replay / Done; remove/restore any grade | Session row | Tarteel Test Mode |

Recitation audio is **muted during a test by default**, following Quran for Android's reasoning. When word timings are available, a counted audio rung on the hint ladder turns audio into a legitimate prompt. Two further unit buttons, waqf segment (Quranki) and mushaf line (Tarteel's question length), fit the same reveal stack and can come later. Shared logic belongs in TypeScript: the reveal state machine, grading, scoring and scheduling. The WebView page only receives `injectJavaScript` state diffs (hide word, reveal word, hide ayah, reveal ayah) and posts back `{surah, ayah, word}` taps. That keeps an eventual iOS port to a WKWebView host.

### Flagged considerations where research pushes against an owner decision

**Binary grading.** Every SRS app and both teacher exam schemes use more than two levels (Anki and FSRS four, Mualim four, exams separating تنبيه from فتح). Pure right/wrong will give a future scheduler less signal. The recommendation keeps the binary verdict exactly as decided and **captures the missing signal passively**: hints, peeks and single-word reveals are recorded per ayah without extra taps. A later FSRS layer can then map ✗ to Again, ✓-with-hints to Hard and clean ✓ to Good. A **strict mode** where any hint auto-sets ✗ matches the "error-free before it counts" rule for new material. I'd make it the default for the new-material tier only.

**Word-by-word translation on a page-exact mushaf.** Inline glosses under each word, Quran.com's "In-line" mode, would reflow the page and break the 604-page layout. For non-Arabic learners, provide meaning and transliteration through the hint ladder and the long-press sheet, which matches Quran.com's "Tooltip" mode ([common.json](https://github.com/quran/quran.com-frontend-next/blob/production/locales/en/common.json)). If inline study is wanted, provide it as a separate non-mushaf view. **The licensing of word-by-word translation and transliteration data was not researched.** The QF API has the fields but cannot be bundled, so this is an open sourcing task.

**No server versus the cleanest licenses.** Only the Quran Foundation has explicit terms. But its no-embedded-secret rule, its one-week cache limit and its ban on prepackaged bundles rule it out for an offline, serverless app. Every remaining source for audio, layout and segments has no published license. A free, ad-free app is a low-risk user of these sources, but "widely used" is not permission. Send written requests to EveryAyah (or islamic.network), to the QUL maintainers (joining issue #772) and to KFGQPC, and credit every source in an About screen.

**Local-only data versus Android Auto Backup.** Auto Backup **stops backing up an app whose data exceeds 25 MB** ([Android Auto Backup](https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup)). Expo's `documentDirectory` sits in the backed-up files directory. So a single downloaded reciter (hundreds of MB) or the ~45 MB font set stored there would silently disable backup of the small database that holds years of mistake history. Exclude the audio and font directories with backup rules (a small Expo config plugin writing `fullBackupContent` / `dataExtractionRules`), keep reference data in its own file outside backup, and add a manual JSON export/import of user data. Reviews about progress lost on reinstall show why that matters.

**Expo SDK 53.** Android background hifz playback through expo-audio realistically needs an SDK upgrade, because of the 3-minute cutoff without lock-screen activation. The upgrade is a prerequisite task, not a detail. Avoid RNTP v5 unless its "commercial" definition is confirmed to exempt a free app.

### A per-ayah attempt log with optional word marks

The schema below follows the Session → Entry → Mistake pattern of Hifz Companion and AHD ([Hifz Companion schema](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shahudtaha08-source/Hifz-companion/main/prisma/schema.prisma)). Positions are keyed by **global ayah id (1–6236) plus 1-based word position, excluding the ayah-end marker**, equivalent to the `surah:ayah:word` location the QF and QUL ecosystems use. Keys never reference page or line, so switching V1 and V2 or adding a 15-line Indo-Pak layout later needs no migration. The attempt log is the immutable source of truth, so a future FSRS or weak-ayat view can replay full history. The stats tables are rebuildable caches.

```sql
-- user.db: small, included in Auto Backup and JSON export
CREATE TABLE meta (key TEXT PRIMARY KEY, value TEXT);          -- schema_version, default mushaf
CREATE TABLE session (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,                    -- uuid
  started_at INTEGER NOT NULL, ended_at INTEGER,
  mushaf TEXT NOT NULL,                   -- 'madani_v2_1421' (layout used, for display only)
  kind TEXT NOT NULL,                     -- 'page_veil' | 'random_prompt'
  tier TEXT,                              -- 'new' | 'recent' | 'old' | 'free' (nullable until plans exist)
  scope_from INTEGER NOT NULL, scope_to INTEGER NOT NULL,   -- ayah ids
  strict INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  ayat_graded INTEGER, ayat_right INTEGER                    -- cached session score
);
CREATE TABLE ayah_attempt (
  id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,
  session_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES session(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  ayah_id INTEGER NOT NULL,               -- 1..6236
  at INTEGER NOT NULL,
  result INTEGER NOT NULL,                -- 1 right, 0 wrong (binary, per owner)
  words_revealed_singly INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  hints INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, max_hint_rung INTEGER,
  peeks INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  duration_ms INTEGER,
  excluded INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0     -- user removed it; restorable (Tarteel pattern)
);
CREATE INDEX ix_attempt_ayah ON ayah_attempt(ayah_id, at);
CREATE TABLE word_mark (
  attempt_id INTEGER NOT NULL REFERENCES ayah_attempt(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  ayah_id INTEGER NOT NULL, pos INTEGER NOT NULL,   -- 1-based, words only
  kind TEXT NOT NULL,                     -- 'wrong' | 'hinted' | 'right' (word drill)
                                          -- reserved: 'missed','added','harakah','tajweed','prompt'
  hint_rung INTEGER,
  confused_with_ayah INTEGER,             -- mutashabih tag
  excluded INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  PRIMARY KEY (attempt_id, pos, kind)
);
CREATE INDEX ix_mark_word ON word_mark(ayah_id, pos);
-- rebuildable caches
CREATE TABLE ayah_stat (ayah_id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY, attempts INTEGER, wrong INTEGER,
  last_at INTEGER, last_wrong_at INTEGER, heat INTEGER /* 0..4 */);
CREATE TABLE word_stat (ayah_id INTEGER, pos INTEGER, wrong INTEGER, hinted INTEGER,
  last_at INTEGER, PRIMARY KEY (ayah_id, pos));
CREATE TABLE daily (date TEXT PRIMARY KEY, ayat_graded INTEGER, ayat_right INTEGER, minutes INTEGER);
```

Session score is `ayat_right ÷ ayat_graded` over non-excluded attempts, matching Tarteel's correct-÷-total. For the mushaf heatmap, a simple proposed rule mirrors Tarteel's described behaviour without its unpublished formula: **heat rises by one per wrong attempt (cap 4) and falls by one per clean right attempt (floor 0)**, and each ayah is tinted by heat on the page. Word tints come from `word_stat.wrong`. Reference data (ayah → surah, page, line, juz; word glyph codes; mutashabihat) lives in a separate read-only database outside backup. The reserved `kind` values and the nullable `tier` column let typed mistakes and sabaq/sabqi/manzil plans arrive later with no schema change.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the risk sits. A non-AI self-test is not a weaker Tarteel. Tarteel's own design shows manual reveal as an afterthought because its business is the recognizer, while its users' loudest complaints (paywalled correction, false flags, mics that stop listening) disappear when the reciter grades themselves. The UI is effectively solved by convergent precedent: two reveal buttons, an undo stack, tap-to-flag and a binary verdict. The owner's decisions fit that precedent almost exactly. The one real correction is that hints and peeks must be logged separately from the verdict, so binary grading today does not starve spaced repetition later.

What is not solved is everything around the page. Rights to the audio and layout data are unpublished, and the one source with clear terms needs a server Zakkir will not run. The Expo SDK must move before background audio is dependable. A 25 MB backup ceiling will quietly sacrifice the user's mistake history the moment audio lands in the wrong directory. Those three items, more than any control layout, decide whether the feature ships safely. They are worth tackling first: permission letters, an SDK upgrade spike, and backup rules written before the first download button exists.
